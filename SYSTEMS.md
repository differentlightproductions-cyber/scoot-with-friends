# Established systems

## Riding and tricks

`src/physics/simulation.ts` owns fixed-step Rapier movement and state. `src/input/riding.ts` owns contextual RS preload/pop, and `src/input/input.ts` owns actual button mapping. Pro uses stance-specific A/X whip/push; Arcade uses A hop, B whip and X push/airborne bars. Preserve both. RS down loads an analog crouch; a completed upward return triggers the hop. Neutral release eases out. LS steers on ground and controls spin/counterweight in air. Stance changes natural whip direction. Fakie preserves backward momentum and scores held time. Pumping/transition launch and coping clearance must preserve entry angle and player intent.

`src/tricks` separates deck, bar, bri and body rotations. Bri/inward follow shoulder-side scooter flips; tailwhip, fingerwhip, barspin, contextual rewinds and kickless retain distinct raw components. Buttercup requires the ordered completed whip/bri/whip sequence. Spin names use accumulated yaw and tolerant 90-degree increments; naming must never snap physics. Live attempts are provisional; completed landings score components, quality, combo and recent-trick variety. Bails discard unbanked points. Held poses (#84): LS leans the rider and scooter together (`Tricks.tweak`, drawn by `RiderModel` via `TWEAK`); during a hand grab (`GRAB_POSES`: Toboggan, Deck Grab, Clamp Grab) a push under `TUNE.grabLeanStick` only leans and a fuller push still spins. The longest hold of each pose (`Tricks.holds`) pays `bodyHoldPoints` per second past `bodyHoldFree`, capped at `bodyHoldMax`. Toboggan (T-bog) is the nose-down tuck from the owner's reference: tail up at the hip with the stance-side hand on it, bars out front, knees up (`TOBOGGAN`); once held, LS leans it rather than starting a Bar Twist. Fastplants (RT+A) work on flat and up ramps too, never with a rail ahead. Grind logic lives in `src/grind`: close contact/intent, no long-distance magnets. Rail exits must not recapture immediately.

## World

Veterans Memorial Park is the flagship outdoor environment. `src/park/outdoor.ts` defines wood transitions (their visuals are built from the same profiles by `src/park/wood-ramps.ts`: plywood, sheathed sides, steel kick plates on every transition; no imported ramp skins); `memorial.ts` defines grounds, metal park and BMX areas. Small box stays x=-4..1, big box left, spine right. Preserve the wood layout. Y toggles walking/riding, LS click runs carrying the scooter. On foot, A into an obstacle vaults it, mantles onto it or climbs it (`Simulation.traversal`: reads terrain and colliders, validates height, headroom, path, landing and standing room, else A is a plain jump; limits in `TUNE.vaultHeight/mantleHeight/climbReach`). Markers are contextual and support drop-in positions. The lake is swimmable (`Simulation.enterWater/swimStep`): riding or walking in parks the ride at the water's edge (`WorldInteractions.parkAtShore`, grabbed back with B like a rack), LS swims (A harder), swimming into the edge climbs out. An on-foot jump with LT+RT and LS up/down is a dive flip; entering the water emits a `swim` event carrying the trick (name, clean, rotations), the hook for a future swimming-tricks game. The first swim plays a sticker and a celebration in the water. Benches support sitting and grindable/rideable surfaces. `src/editor` is explicitly IN TESTING.

Weather (`src/park/weather.ts`, #85) runs on every open-air map (`WEATHER_MAPS`: Veterans, B Hill, the Church) from the one Weather setting; the Warehouse and the shop are indoors. The ground state (snow depth, wetness, leaf litter, what is falling) carries across map changes, so the city keeps one sky. Maps declare roofed `Shelter`s in `scene.userData.shelters` (church building and plaza canopy, Veterans pavilions): nothing falls, settles, soaks or gathers under them. `tests/weather-maps.browser.mjs` covers all three maps.

Outdoor scenery is generated by `src/art` (no downloaded models or photos): `sky.ts` (SkyDome: gradient, sun, clouds, stars, weather overcast; renders the lighting environment, `daylight.ts` eases it between phases and ties fog to its horizon), `desert.ts` (desert floor and mountain ranges around a map's keep-out footprint, an optional landscaped rock apron, and `scatterDesert`), `flora.ts` (creosote, bursage, Aleppo pine, fan palm, yucca, grass, boulders as merged parts drawn with InstancedMesh, wind sway in the vertex shader; `plant(..., thin=true)` scatter thins with fidelity via `userData.scatterCount`), `textures.ts` (canvas-painted ground, gravel, scrub, rock, bark and leaf cards) and `noise.ts` (seeded simplex/fbm/ridged). Reuse this kit for every outdoor map.

B Hill (`src/park/bhill.ts`) is one centreline driving road, collision and heights. Its grade is keyed per section (10% top rising to 16.5% in the final fast sections, with easings before tight corners) and the runout climbs. Speed comes only from slope: past `pushMaxSpeed` the scooter meets air drag (`scooterAero`), a tyre grip ceiling (`scooterGripAccel`, holding full lock washes the front out) and a shared speed wobble fed by quick outward steering, tyre slip and loose ground (`terrainSurface`). None of these act at or below pushing speed, so park riding is unchanged. The longboard keeps its own lean/carve/aero model and shares the wobble. `tests/bhill.browser.mjs` runs controlled and aggressive descents for both rides. The longboard (#87) is a racer there: its tuck runs to about 36 m/s on the steepest sections (`boardTuckAero`), and at bombing speed it carves as hard as a scooter corners (`boardRaceCarveAccel`, blended in above pushing speed so park carving is unchanged), with its speed wobble starting 3 m/s later than the scooter's.

B Hill's road (#89) follows the owner's aerial photo: the blue line's curves traced at 0.75 m a pixel (about 1.49 km with the staging pad and runout), its tightest bends where the photo has them (radius ~65-90 m at 530 and 1040 m), and the grade keys re-placed so the easings lead into those bends while the section profile (top < mid < lower < final) is unchanged. Two side streets (`B_HILL_STREETS`) leave on the riders' left where the photo's red and yellow lines do, toward Veterans Memorial Park and the church; each climbs gently, is paved and rideable, and ends at a barricade inside the corridor until the continuous city (#43) carries it on, with a guide sign 60 m up the hill. Houses line both sides (`sites()`, about 105): each lot (`planHouse` in `src/art/houses.ts`) is a ranch, two-storey or white flat-roofed pueblo house (parapets, vigas, a portal), with a garage wing, concrete-tile, barrel-tile or metal roof, solar panels, AC units, a car on the drive or an RV on its own pad, and a block-walled backyard with a patio (often a pergola), frequently a pool, turf, a shed or a trampoline. The yard is levelled a little above the street and the garage goes on whichever end keeps the driveway under a 21% grade. The ground under every lot belongs to the height function (`lotGround`, applied only beyond the shoulder, so the shoulder is one flush surface the whole way down, past the side streets' mouths too): just under the yard, the driveway slab exactly (driveways, patios, pool decks and side streets read as `road`), the pool's floor, and a cut bank back to the hillside that never falls faster than 0.9 from the shoulder's edge, so yards, driveways and pools are walkable and rideable. Each driveway starts at the shoulder's outer edge and twists as it falls (`tilt`) so its foot meets a street that runs downhill across it, with a draped concrete apron over the shoulder; parked cars sit on the twist. The road's collision ribbon drops to a pool's floor around each pool, so its 2 m triangles never ramp up the pool walls (walk in, A at the wall climbs out); the retaining pad is split round the driveway notch and the pool (`cellsAround`) for its colliders; walls, houses and vehicles are solid. Houses merge per material per 160 m chunk, so the view and the shadow pass cull by chunk; windows, cars, turf and pool water are each chunk's distance detail (`scene.userData.detailChunks`, switched by `VisualFidelity.update`: 90/160/230 m by preset). Vehicles are built in `src/art/vehicles.ts` (sedan, SUV, pickup, class C motorhome, travel trailer).

Streets and curbs (#87, `src/park/streets.ts`): a `StreetPlan` sinks streets and lots a curb (15 cm) below the park, as real ones are, and answers everything from one description (street and island rectangles, curb ramps/driveways, walks): the height offset `terrainHeight` adds (outdoor.ts), the curbs traced round the streets and islands, flat collider cuboids per level, the ground and lawn covers with the streets cut out, and the asphalt, gutter pans, curb, walk, ramp and tactile-panel meshes. A curb is a wall to the wheels (`Simulation.meetWall` checks the leading wheel with `curbCrossed`, since the wheelbase average would split the step): faster into it than `curbBailSpeed` throws the rider, slower stops them, a glancing touch turns the wheel along it; ramps, hops and riding off it work normally. Veterans' lot, drive aisle and two streets use it (`VETERANS_STREETS` in memorial.ts); B Hill keeps flush rolled gutters, as the photo's street has.

Gutters and storm drains (`src/park/gutters.ts`, `Drainage`, stepped from main.ts with the weather): each gutter is a flowline with inlets. Runoff spreads and runs by Manning's equation for a triangular gutter (spread with flow^3/8, speed with flow^1/4), accumulating down each run; an inlet on a grade takes 70% and the rest runs on, a sag takes all (Veterans' flat streets get a designed fall to each inlet; B Hill's run downhill with an inlet pair every 80 m and a sag at the bottom). Fall leaves lie along the curbs and rain washes them to the grates, where half go down and half mat on the grate and pond water round it. Snow builds a bank at the curb; packing while it snows and meltwater refreezing at night turn the gutter to ice that outlasts the snow and thaws by day; `iceAt` takes 80% of the tyres' and wheels' grip there (scooter carve, grip ceiling, wash-out and brake; longboard `traction`). Runoff, bank and ice carry across maps like the weather.

Light and weather at night: only the sun casts full-scene shadows (it follows the rider in whole shadow texels, `render/fidelity.ts`). Park lamps (all sodium amber) light static materials through the weather material hook (`art/particle-light.ts` `parkLampLight`, set by `daylight.ts` from the 8 lamps nearest the rider) instead of painted pools; the rider's headlamp is one plain SpotLight, present on every map. Weather particles are night-lit by `lightParticles()`. The moon (`art/moon.ts`) has real phase and, with live sky, real position. `park/dust.ts` adds dust devils and blowing dust; `park/doves.ts` is the one wildlife system (mourning doves). Keep the scene at three lights (sun, sky, headlamp): every shader pays for each one.

Racks, persistent consumables, fountain drinking, and day/night controls are integrated. Warehouse has its existing quick builder. Techno Gravity is a separate lazy-loaded walkable shop and DIY spot; physical displays open the shared catalog and atomic Credit transaction flow.

Vending machines (#88, `src/park/vending.ts`) follow the owner's reference photo: a black cabinet, a lit glass door over six shelves of spiral coils holding the park's own products (Crunch Ridge chips, Clear Mesa water, Voltade sports drinks, Fizz Pop cans; `VEND_SLOTS`, codes row then column, 11 to 65, priced in Coins), and a control column with a yellow tap-to-pay reader, a green display, a note acceptor, a 4 x 3 keypad, a lock and a coin return over a steel PUSH flap. The static model is merged per material (about 20 draws, one atlas for all product art); within 26 m every product sits in its coil, beyond that a painted card stands in (`VendingMachine.lod`). B at a machine starts a `VendingSession`: the rider stands at the column with the scooter let go beside them, a camera frames the machine over their shoulder, LS moves over the keypad and A presses a key (the hand goes to it); two digits select a coil (C clears, a code not on the shelves says so). X (or OK) holds the phone flat to the reader (`PhoneRig.pose` with a hold, `Phone.payScreen`): its lights come on, the wallet is charged in one transaction (`CreditEconomy.spend`) and it beeps approved, or buzzes declined and the phone asks to add funds (offered later, shown disabled). Approved, the coil turns the front product off the shelf, it falls behind the lower door into the bin with a thunk, and A pushes the flap in and takes it into the pockets and the hand. What drops is a copy of the front product, so the shelves never run out. B leaves. Sounds are synthesised and placed at the machine (`AudioEngine.vend`). `tests/vending.test.ts`, `tests/vending.browser.mjs`.

## Social

`src/phone/`: the rider's phone replaced the old radial wheel (docs/briefs/PHONE-SYSTEM.md). Holding D-pad Down (`PHONE_HOLD`, 0.55 s, `hold.ts`) takes it out standing, sitting or rolling on the ground (never in the air, a trick, a grind, a manual or a crash) and holding it again puts it away; a tap never opens it, and inside the phone a tap steps down a list. The phone owns controller input while out (`phoneStep` in main.ts: LS/D-pad move, A select, B back, Y home) and gameplay receives an empty frame, so a rider coasts; buttons still held when it closes are ignored until released (`Input.clear`). One state machine and app registry (`phone.ts`), one canvas-drawn screen (`canvas-ui.ts`) shown in a hand-held bezel in third person and as the 3D phone's screen texture in first person, where a close-up pass (`rig.ts`, render layer 5, narrower lens from the same eye) keeps it readable without changing the world FOV. Apps (`apps.ts`) are front ends to existing systems: Sesh Music, the emote system, loadout/ride switching, the avatar creator, an overhead map photo with live markers (`map.ts`), pockets, the Warehouse builder and chat (`messages.ts`, fictional 702-555-01xx numbers). Vending machines are worked on the machine itself (#88); the phone only shows its pay card while held to the reader and, after a decline, an add-funds sheet. Pausing or crashing stows it. Settings: Phone hand (left/right) and notifications.

Phone home screen (#86): the time and date widget, then a 4 x 4 grid of apps that fills the screen (`HOME_COLS`/`HOME_ROWS`); no page buttons. LS past the grid's edge turns the page, a swipe does on touch. X rearranges: A picks the focused app up, LS carries it (a row at a time up and down), A puts it down, X is done; a long press starts it on touch and a tap drops the held app there. The order is saved.

Phone camera (#77, `src/phone/camera.ts`): OPEN CAMERA in the CAMERA app holds the phone to the eye: the view goes first person (restored after) with an HTML viewfinder over it. A takes a photo, X starts and stops a clip (up to `MAX_CLIP` 60 s, with the world's sound from `AudioEngine.recordStream`), LB / RB zoom the lens (`ChaseCamera.zoom`), Y opens the gallery on the phone, B puts it down; the sticks and brake still move the rider, and opening the phone, pausing, a crash, a map change or the replay editor put it down. Photos and clips are the canvas only, grabbed right after the frame renders (`afterRender`), so the HUD never appears in them. They, and saved replays (`ReplayStore`, #41), live in memory for the session: nothing is written to the browser (the IndexedDB replay library earlier builds kept is deleted), so there is no storage limit, and reopening the game starts empty. SAVE TO DEVICE (and the replay editor's EXPORT VIDEO) hand the file over through `saveToDevice` in `src/render/video.ts`: a download, or the share sheet when tapped on a phone.

`src/ui/social.ts`: emotes (started from the phone), chat (hold D-pad Right or MESSAGES) and the speech bubble. Movement interrupts emotes. Each emote declares `hands` (none/one/two), `phoneCompatible` and `preferredHand` (oppositePhone/left/right/either): one-hand and head emotes play with the phone still out, on the hand that is not holding it; two-hand and full-body emotes put the phone away first. The HUD minimap (`src/ui/minimap.ts`, top-left, turns with the camera, compass ring, ride-type marker) draws the MAP app's photo and features (`PhoneMap.drawMini`), so ramps, vending, shops and friends are one data source. Private-room chat is relayed by `src/network/client.ts` when connected.

## Customization

`src/avatar/` (`config.ts`, `rig.ts`, `face.ts`, `parts.ts`, `avatar.ts`): the rider avatar (docs/AVATAR-DESIGN.md). A rider is a small saved configuration (`profile.avatar`), validated by `sanitizeAvatar` locally and in the multiplayer appearance message; meshes are rebuilt from it. One 16-bone skeleton for every rider: body types (slim/standard/stocky) change girth only; limbs are rigid rounded segments placed by fixed-length two-bone IK and never stretch. `rig.ts` owns every body dimension the pose drivers use (shoulder and hip joints, segment lengths, ankle height, riding and standing stance). Faces are painted from the configuration onto a front decal; hair, headwear and eyewear share one head-surface function. `src/data/scooterParts.ts`, `loadout.ts`: authored products/colorways and the saved profile. Lazer and Mafioso parts use the same catalog, ownership and loadout. Confirmed rewards grant Credit (shown to players as Coins) through the configured reward policy. Bucks (`wallet.bucks`, the premium currency) are earned 5 every 5 levels and buy Mafioso parts; buying Bucks with money stays disabled (docs/PAYMENTS-PLAN.md). Version 3 browser-local profiles migrate older saves (pre-avatar gear maps once to the nearest avatar items) and add `pockets` without changing the storage key. Phones and PCs have separate saves.

`src/data/items.ts` stores specific consumable identities and sealed/opened/empty states. `src/park/interactions.ts` owns item use and fountain actions and starts vending sessions (`src/park/vending.ts`); render props never create inventory entries. Backpack is cosmetic and labels the same Pockets inventory. On-foot Use Held Item is remappable (X default). Mount and bail stow items, and interrupted actions retain their completed consumption markers.

`src/player/crash.ts` owns two temporary CCD bodies for rider and assembled scooter during bail. Riding forces and IK release once; stable contact and low motion permit settling. A requests clearance-tested local recovery on real terrain/platforms. Invalid crash motion falls back locally or to last safe ground, not routinely to spawn. `src/player/body-flip.ts` uses bounded analog target rates, integrated pitch and independent yaw; neutral slows an established flip, opposing input brakes and released modifiers damp. Only eligible takeoffs unlock flips. The final camera rule is mounted RS always riding/tricks, on-foot RS camera. L3 Run and R3 Recenter remain intact.

## Progression, shop and accounts

`src/data/progress.ts`: XP, levels, missions and crates, all inside the saved profile (`profile.progress`) and changed only through `CreditEconomy` transactions (`credit.ts`: `track`, `openCrate`, `buy`, with the `swf-alpha-wallet` lock), so Credit, XP and crates land together or not at all. `src/ui/rewards.ts` counts riding events into mission stats (`MissionTracker`) and plays the results (`RewardFx`: mission stickers, level-up slam, level chip, the tap-tap-tap crate opening); the phone MISSIONS app lists crates, daily and career missions and the collection. Levels need 200 + 75·(level − 1) XP. Career missions are 12 staged chains; three daily missions are seeded by the date. Crates (Street and Legend since #76; old Pro/Signature crates migrate) are seeded by their id, so a reopened crate can never re-roll, and never grant an owned item (a duplicate pays Credit instead). Crate-exclusive colourways (`PartVariant.exclusive`, ids `x-…`) are never sold. `src/data/deals.ts`: three daily deals per shop, seeded by shop and day, so the economy re-derives a deal price rather than trusting the menu, and refuses a purchase whose price rose. Shop screens (`menu.ts`, `shop.css`): wallet strip, deals, rarity-framed shelves with collection meters, the counter and the SOLD screen.

`server/auth-api.ts`, `src/ui/account.ts`, `src/data/cloud.ts`: game accounts and cloud save. While signed in the whole saved profile is copied to the account (`game_saves`, drizzle migration 0001, 64 KB limit) about 20 s after a change and when the tab is hidden; it always stays on the device too. Each device remembers the account revision it last matched and the server only accepts uploads based on its current revision, so two devices never silently overwrite each other: an unchanged device pulls the account copy, and when both changed the player picks one in the account dialog (the replaced device save is kept under `lazer-profile-before-cloud-v1`). Signing out stops syncing and keeps progress on the device. Cloud saves are a convenience copy of client-side state; any future real-money entitlement must be granted and checked on the server, never read from a save.

Crate result previews (#53, #78, `GameMenu.renderPartPreview` / `rewardProduct` in `src/ui/menu.ts`): the revealed part is lifted out on its own and the camera is fitted to its bounds, so a bushing reads as big as a deck. Scooter parts use the shop's isolated product (one grip of the pair); board parts are cut from the assembled longboard by part id with `pickTriangles`: the front truck's trucks, bushings or hardware, one wheel's wheel or bearings, the deck on its edge showing its graphic, the grip on its deck. UI palettes (`src/ui/palette.ts`) recolor interface only; in grayscale and earth the selection colour is a clear mid tone and the accent text colour stays dark enough to read on paper.

B Hill signs (#96, owner request): no banners. Real street signs stand on the riders' right, clear of driveways and side streets (`art/road-signs.ts`): a green B HILL street-name blade at the top, SPEED LIMIT 25, a hill warning with a STEEP GRADE plaque, a curve warning ahead of each real bend (found from the route's heading, a reverse-curve sign for the S-bends) and the B HILL / PUEBLO DR cross street at the bottom. The checkered START and FINISH gates are built hidden and stand only during a timed race: `setRaceGates(scene, on)` in `park/bhill.ts`, for the race lifecycle (#81 stage 3) to call. Road cracks and oil stains are sparse marks of their own (`art/road-marks.ts`); the asphalt tile carries none, so nothing repeats.

B Hill life (#80 audit, #21): a wooden power line runs down the riders' left (`art/utility-poles.ts` `utilityLine`): poles about 40 m apart, closer on bends so each span stays over the verge and street, clear of driveways, mailboxes, signs and side streets, a can transformer on every fifth pole, three primaries and a communications cable sagging between them. Poles, hardware and transformers are instanced (three draw calls), the wires are two merged meshes, each pole has a collider. Green pad-mounted transformers and telecom pedestals (`utilityBoxes`, instanced) stand on the right where the verge is level and off the lots. The one dove system (`park/doves.ts`) runs on B Hill too: perches are the wires, crossarm ends, roof ridges and yard wall caps (`buildHouses` returns `perches`; `scene.userData.dovePerches`), and they land on shoulders and open yards. `DoveWorld.roam`: on a long map a dove more than 150 m from the rider, out of sight, moves to a perch 45 to 110 m around them, so six birds keep up with a rider bombing the hill; unset at Veterans. Scatter sits on what is drawn: `groundAt` interpolates the hillside band's and skirt's own vertices and triangles (not the smooth height function), and yard trees keep off pools.

## Architecture and performance


Loading (#85, `loadDestination`/`warmUp` in main.ts) finishes all GPU work behind the loading screen, with the bar following it: models placed (n/N), static batching, one real frame for sky/fog/weather, every texture uploaded, every shader compiled (weather particles included), four real frames looking each way, the minimap photo. Nothing compiles or uploads after the screen closes (`tests/loading-warm.browser.mjs`). `render/static-batch.ts` merges static meshes of one look into a mesh per 40 m cell (Veterans: about 965 meshes into 192, about 39% fewer draw calls); characters, doves, throwables, vending machines and trash cans stay whole, shared or glowing materials keep their own batch. Local riders (`social/npcs.ts`, `NPC_DETAIL`) drop tiny parts and shadows past 25 m and are not drawn or posed past 70 m. Per-map lists on `scene.userData` are cleared when the scene is rebuilt. Veterans' ground (`park/terrain-lod.ts`): the 12.5 cm sampled surface was 720,896 triangles in one mesh; flat, single-surface 4 m tiles now draw from a 1 m grid (edges that meet detailed tiles keep every point and fan in, so it stays watertight) in 24 chunks the camera culls, 310,080 triangles, about 35% fewer per frame, same pixels (`tests/terrain-lod.test.ts`). Collision and the owner's base editor still use the full surface (`park.terrainGeometry`).
Boulder City, NV east grounds (#100, owner's street views and aerial photo; supersedes the #99 "north" placement). The owner's photo is turned a quarter to the game's compass: photo-north is game east (+x). The field, pond and lake lie EAST of the skatepark and the BMX track, across Buchanan (the east street, which again ends at z -99). `VETERANS_BOUNDS` is x -116..372, z -170..80: the park's rectangle plus the east grounds. `LAKESIDE_DRIVE` (memorial.ts) is the centreline of a 7 m flat asphalt ribbon (Catmull-Rom, sampled every 2 m; dashed centre line, white edge lines; the ground slab is its collider) from a stub street opposite the lot's drive aisle (z -65) winding north-east up to the field, round its north, east and south sides and back to Buchanan opposite the BMX track (z -25); each stub crosses the far walk with curb ramps and rises to the grounds' level with a `streetEnd` ramp at x 112..115. `EAST_FIELD` (x 186..262, z -104..-28) is lawn for a future soccer pitch. `water.ts` `LAKES` keeps the photo-traced outlines and turns them (`turn`); the lake is 3.2 m deep and the bowl is steeper (exponent 0.5) so the dive dock, now at the lake's north tip running south, has about 1.9 m under the board. Trees line both sides of the drive and ring the water. The out-of-bounds reset (`outOfVeterans` in simulation.ts) follows `VETERANS_BOUNDS`; the old hard-coded x ±113 / z -166 limits were the "invisible barrier" the owner hit. The desert keep-out is the park rectangle plus the east grounds, with mow curbs round both. Only the Boulder City map is on the Maps list and the phone's fast-travel list (`SPOTS`; the others are in `HIDDEN_SPOTS`); the phone BUILD app goes straight to the Warehouse.

Distance detail (#98, owner request: full detail only near the rider). Three layers, all scaled by the preset (`FidelityManager.lodScale`: low 0.65, medium 0.82, high 1): instanced scenery (plants, rocks, B Hill poles, hardware and utility boxes) registers with `render/instance-lod.ts`, which keeps each mesh's full instance list and draws only the instances within its range of the rider (plants 160 to 520 m by size, pole hardware 140 m, transformers 320 m, boxes 220 m), repacked every 6 m of travel rather than every frame, with the bounds shrunk to what is drawn so the camera and the shadow pass cull the rest; small static meshes (under 0.45 m across: bolts, caps, labels, trim) batch separately per 40 m cell in `static-batch.ts` and hide past 50/72/100 m (`scene.userData.smallDetail`); and the B Hill house detail chunks keep their 90/160/230 m ranges. The preset's scenery density still picks the leading share of the pre-shuffled instances. The phone map and the loading warm-up call `showAllInstances` (every material compiles behind the loading screen; the overhead photo shows the whole map) and repack afterwards.

Laptop performance (#100, owner: "really chunky all around"). The minimap no longer retakes a rider-centred photo every ~50 m on a large map (each retake re-rendered the whole scene with every instance shown and stalled on the pixel read-back): a map over 300 m gets one 2048 px photo, used wherever it is sharp enough (at least 3.4 px/m); the minimap redraws at most 10 times a second and only when the rider moved or the view turned (or once a second). The HUD writes text and styles only when they change instead of every frame. High renders at 1.15x the screen's ratio instead of 1.35x. While riding, a machine held under 30 fps for about 8 s steps down one preset (High to Medium, then Low), saves it and says so; automated browsers (`navigator.webdriver`) are left alone.

`src/main.ts` orchestrates input, simulation, rendering, menu, audio, editor and local social. `src/scooter` builds procedural rider and parts. Three.js instancing/LOD support the landscape. Keep render loops allocation-light and avoid many shadow lights. `server/worker.ts` provides the optional authenticated park-layout publishing API. `portable` provides a local-only server; credentials are unnecessary for normal play.
